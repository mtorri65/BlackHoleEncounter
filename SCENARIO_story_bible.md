# Story bible — *A black hole crosses the solar system*

A working reference for writing fiction in this scenario. Everything here comes
from the simulation `20260811_184731__rp0p75__vinf25__inc30__toff59132__Om0__om30`
unless marked otherwise.

The other documents in this repository are written for someone continuing the
analysis. This one is written for someone writing a book: it front-loads the
constraints you can't break, the dates you can build scenes around, and what a
person standing on the ground would actually experience. It also marks clearly
where the simulation stops determining things and you take over.

---

## 1. The premise, in brief

A black hole of **0.1 solar masses** — a tenth the Sun's mass, and about **600
metres across** — falls through the solar system on a hyperbolic orbit at
**25 km/s**. It arrives from the direction of Taurus, rounds the Sun at
**0.662 AU on 20 August 2047**, and leaves toward Aquarius.

It does not hit anything. It does not need to. It passes close enough that its
gravity rewrites the orbits of every planet, and then it is gone.

**Earth survives, and is ruined.** It is flung outward to 1.49 AU on a markedly
eccentric orbit and freezes solid within about four years. It keeps the Moon.
**Mercury leaves with the black hole**, captured into orbit around it.
**Saturn is thrown out** to a 3,244 AU aphelion. **Venus is pushed outward into
Earth's vacated orbit.** Mars is nudged inward and, for the first time in its
history, becomes marginally habitable.

The crucial structural fact: **this is not a surprise.** The object is
gravitationally detectable from around 1900 and unambiguous within a generation.
Humanity watches it come for roughly a century and a half, with the arrival date
calculable to the day long before it matters.

## 2. Hard constraints

Break these and the scenario stops being this scenario.

**The black hole is invisible.** It emits nothing. It is 591 m across at a
minimum distance of 1.29 AU — an angular size of 6×10⁻⁴ arcseconds, roughly a
thousandth of the finest detail any telescope has ever resolved. **Nobody ever sees
it.** No dark disc against the stars, no shadow, no ominous sphere. What people
observe are its *effects*: first perturbations in planetary orbits, later the
gravitational lensing of stars that pass behind it.

**It is not a surprise.** Detection begins with residuals in Uranus's position
and is unambiguous by the early 20th century. By 1930 it is characterised, by the
1960s planetary ranging has settled the argument, and from about 1990 three
independent channels agree. **Any plot beat that depends on humanity being
ambushed is unavailable.** The story is about living with a known date.

**Earth freezes; it is not destroyed, shattered, or swallowed.** No tidal
disruption, no crust cracking, no dramatic tides. It moves to a colder orbit and
the temperature falls over years. That is all — and it is enough.

**The Moon stays.** It remains bound to Earth throughout; the lunar orbit is
essentially unchanged (386,011 km against today's 384,400). Earth's Hill radius
at its new orbit is still 4.4× the lunar distance.

**Tides barely change during the passage.** At closest approach the black hole's
tidal effect on Earth is **2.1% of the Moon's**. If you want catastrophic
tides, this object cannot give them to you. Its direct pull is 6% of the Sun's —
enough to reshape an orbit over months, nowhere near enough to tear anything.

| **2046** | 11 AU | Still inside the giant planets, and the planets' *orbital elements* still read close to normal — the disruption is not yet expressed. |

### Act II — The passage (May 2047 – November 2048)

This is the compressed part. Nineteen months, most of it in six weeks.

| when | what |
|---|---|
| **10 May 2047** | Jupiter at 4.92 AU |
| **28 June 2047** | Mars at 0.810 AU |
| **20 Aug 2047** | **Perihelion, 0.662 AU.** Position RA 11.4ʰ, Dec +1.9° — on the Leo/Virgo border |
| **26 Aug 2047** | **Mercury changes hands** — captured by the black hole |
| **28 Aug – 11 Sept 2047** | **Venus, Earth and the Moon.** Closest to Earth 10 Sept, **1.291 AU**, in Virgo (RA 13.6ʰ, Dec −14.6°) |

### Act III — Consequence (2049–2100)

- **Earth freezes in about four years.** Ice reaches the equator; equilibrium at
  **205 K (−68 °C)**.
- **Mars has a season.** Pulled inward to 1.336 AU with eccentricity 0.217; liquid
  water becomes possible for **7.2% of the year at latitude −21.5°**.
- By **2100** the system has settled into its new, unstable arrangement.

## 4. What people actually see

**Before ~2040 — nothing at all.** No naked-eye phenomenon, nothing in an
amateur telescope. The object exists only as numbers: a discrepancy in where
Uranus is, then a predicted track. For 150 years the most consequential object in
human history is a table of residuals. *This is the best material in the whole
scenario and it is entirely psychological.*

**The lensing signature grows slowly, then quickly.** A background star passing
directly behind the black hole is smeared into an Einstein ring:

| year | distance | Einstein ring | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1885 | 863 AU | 0.44″ | |
| 1950 | 522 AU | 0.57″ | |
| 2000 | 257 AU | 0.81″ | |
| **2018** | **162 AU** | **1.02″** | **Gaia DR2 — it becomes a point on the sky** |
| 2030 | 97 AU | 1.31″ | |
| 2045 | 16 AU | 3.25″ | |
| **Sept 2047** | **1.29 AU** | **11.4″** | |

**2018 is the beat worth using.** Gaia — a real mission, flying now — is what
first converts the object from a direction into a *position*. The trick is that
you do not need a star to pass behind it: weak deflection falls off slowly, so a
background star a full degree away is still displaced by **288 µas against
Gaia's 25 µas precision**. The result is a coherent astrometric distortion
field tens of degrees across, dragged over the sky by the object's parallax.

Everything before this is inference from how the planets move. After it, there
is a spot in the sky you can point at.

**By 2047 it is unmistakable.** At closest approach the ring is **11.4
arcseconds** — comfortably resolvable. Stars within that radius of its position
are visibly doubled or drawn into arcs.
For a few weeks in 2047 there is a patch of sky where the stars are *wrong*: a
moving, invisible centre around which background stars split, brighten, wheel and
rejoin. **This is the one directly observable phenomenon, and it is strange
rather than spectacular** — you need a telescope, and what you see is a distortion
rather than an object.

**The path across the sky.** It enters in Taurus near the Auriga border and
creeps for a century and a half — a fraction of a degree per year, mostly
disguised as parallax loops. Then it accelerates: **0.056°/day in 2045, 0.086° in
2046, 0.193° in 2047**, peaking at **1.78°/day on 4 September 2047**. For scale,
the Moon covers about 13°/day. So even at its fastest it crawls compared to the
Moon — but it crosses the sky in weeks after taking a century to cross a
constellation.

Its route: **Taurus/Auriga → Gemini → Cancer → Leo → Virgo → Libra → Scorpius →
Sagittarius → Capricornus → Aquarius**, moving right to left as seen on a star
chart. (Rendered tracks and animations exist in this repository:
`bh_sky_track_full.png`, `bh_sky_track_2045_2051.mp4`.)

**On the ground during the passage: nothing dramatic.** No sensation, no tides,
no sky-filling object. The most consequential fortnight in Earth's history looks
like an ordinary fortnight. What changes is measurable only by instrument — until
the seasons start arriving wrong.

## 5. When it gets a name

The scenario documents call it a black hole from the first line. **Nobody in the
story gets to.** The gap between knowing what the object does and having a word
for what it is runs to about thirty years, and it is the strongest structure
available in Act I.

**Stage 1 — the 1930s: the facts.** Mass and distance are disentangled, the
approach speed measured at 25 km/s against a local escape speed of 1.7 km/s.
The object is unbound, it is arriving, and the arrival is dated to the middle of
the twenty-first century, ±18 years. Everything quantitative is settled.

**Stage 2 — immediately after: it is dark.** This step is available the moment
mass and direction are both known, and it is decisive. A 0.1 M☉ object on the
main sequence is an M6 dwarf. At 628 AU it would shine at **V = −0.6, brighter
than Sirius** — one of the most conspicuous objects in the sky.

| year | distance | how bright an ordinary 0.1 M☉ star would look |
|---|---|---|
| 1885 | 863 AU | V = +0.1 |
| 1930 | 628 AU | **V = −0.6** |
| 1960 | 470 AU | V = −1.2 |
| 2018 | 162 AU | V = −3.5 |

They have a direction good to arcminutes. They look, and find **nothing** — not
faint, absent. The object is therefore dark and compact, and that conclusion is
inescapable in the 1930s.

**Stage 3 — the 1960s: there is finally a category.** And this is the payload.
In the 1930s no physics permits what they are holding:

- **"Black hole" is not coined until 1967** (Wheeler). Oppenheimer–Snyder
  gravitational collapse is 1939 — *after* the object is characterised.
- **0.1 M☉ is sub-Chandrasekhar.** It cannot be a stellar remnant by any route:
  the collapse channel needs several solar masses. So even once collapse is
  understood, this object does not fit.
- It requires **primordial black holes** — proposed by Zel'dovich & Novikov
  (1966) and developed by Hawking (1971).

So the object is fully characterised roughly three decades before there is a
theory that allows it to exist, and when the name finally arrives it carries an
unsettling implication: this thing is a relic of the first second of the
universe, older than every atom in the solar system, and it has been falling
toward the Sun the entire time.

*(Stages 1 and 3 are historically real dates for real physics. Stage 2's
brightness figures are computed for this scenario. What a 1930s astronomer would
actually have concluded, argued about, or refused to believe is yours — see §8.)*

## 6. The world afterwards

### Earth

- Semi-major axis **1.000 → 1.486 AU**, eccentricity **0.017 → 0.236**.
- Year lengthens to **662 days**.
- Equilibrium **205 K (−68 °C)**; the ocean freezes; ice reaches the equator by
  roughly year 2 of the decline, equilibrium in about four.
- Obliquity almost unchanged (23.5° → 23.9°) — seasons keep their tilt, but the
  eccentricity now dominates. The planet swings between 1.13 and 1.84 AU each
  year, so "seasons" become a distance effect, not a tilt effect.
- **The Moon is still there**, unchanged.

### The sky from Earth

- **The Sun is smaller and moves**: it shrinks from 32′ to between 28.2′ (at
  perihelion) and 17.4′ (at aphelion). Over one 662-day year the Sun visibly
  changes size by a factor of 1.6.
- **Every solar eclipse is now total.** The Moon's apparent size is unchanged, so
  it always exceeds the shrunken Sun. Annular eclipses become impossible.
- **Totality lasts roughly 50 minutes** (against today's 7.5 maximum), and the
  shadow can be **1,675 km wide** — wider than today's by a factor of ten.
- Nobody is left to watch them.

### The other planets

| | after | note |
|---|---|---|
| **Mercury** | gone | captured by the black hole |
| **Venus** | 0.989 AU, e 0.200 | inherits Earth's old orbit; insolation drops 45% |
| **Mars** | 1.336 AU, e 0.217 | the habitability window |
| **Jupiter** | 5.00 AU, e 0.169 | least disturbed |
| **Saturn** | 1,627 AU, e 0.994 | aphelion 3,244 AU, period 65,600 years — effectively expelled |
| **Uranus** | 11.49 AU, e 0.679 | driven *inward*, now crosses Jupiter's orbit |
| **Neptune** | 29.9 AU, e 0.515 | keeps its distance, gains huge eccentricity |

### The system is not stable

Seven of the twenty-one planet pairs now have overlapping orbits — every pair in
the inner system, plus Uranus crossing three of the four surviving giants. This
arrangement will keep evolving through close encounters over 10⁵–10⁶ years. The
simulation only runs 316 years, so **nothing beyond about 2200 is established.**
Mars's habitability window is real but not shown to be durable.

## 8. Where you have license

The simulation determines orbits and temperatures. It says nothing whatever
about:

- **Anything human.** Politics, institutions, whether the warning is believed,
  what a civilisation does with 150 years of notice, who leaves and who stays.
- **Whether anyone survives, and how.** Underground, in orbit, on Mars, not at
  all — all equally consistent with the physics.
- **Mars colonisation.** The window is real; whether it is reachable, usable, or
  worth the attempt is entirely yours. Note it is *marginal* — 7% of a year at
  one latitude, not a garden.
- **Venus.** The simulation moves it into Earth's orbit and stops. Whether a
  92-bar runaway greenhouse cools when its sunlight drops 45% is genuinely
  unknown — the climate model in this project cannot answer it, and I would not
  trust anyone who says it obviously can. **This is free space, and it is the
  most interesting free space in the scenario.**
- **The 1885–2000 detection history.** The physics says it was detectable; the
  actual sequence of astronomers, disputes, and institutions is invention.
- **Anything after ~2200.**

## 9. Things that would be wrong

Common instincts that this scenario does not support:

- ❌ *Someone sees a black disc / dark star approaching.* It is invisible.
- ❌ *Earth is discovered to be doomed shortly before impact.* 150 years of warning.
- ❌ *Catastrophic tides, earthquakes, crustal heating during the passage.* Tides
  are 2% of the Moon's.
- ❌ *Earth is swallowed, shattered, or torn apart.* It is moved, and it freezes.
- ❌ *The Moon is ripped away.* It stays, verified explicitly.
- ❌ *The black hole "hits" or grazes something.* Closest approach to Earth is
  1.29 AU — 500 times the Earth–Moon distance.
- ❌ *Instant catastrophe.* Earth takes about four years to freeze.
- ❌ *Mars becomes an Eden.* A marginal window, one latitude, part of the year.
- ❌ *A stable new solar system afterwards.* Seven crossing orbit pairs.
